How stamp duty works in NSW

In NSW, "stamp duty" is officially called transfer duty. It's a one-off tax you pay when you buy a property or land, charged on the dutiable value (usually the purchase price or market value, whichever is higher). It's normally payable within 3 months of exchanging contracts, and your conveyancer usually arranges it at settlement.

First home buyer concessions (FHBAS)

Under the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme, eligible first home buyers get:

  • New or existing home up to $800,000 — no transfer duty (full exemption).
  • $800,000 to $1,000,000 — a reduced concessional rate.
  • Vacant land up to $350,000 — exempt; $350,000–$450,000 — concessional.

Newly built or off-the-plan homes may also qualify for the $10,000 First Home Owner Grant. To be eligible you generally must move in within 12 months and live there for at least 12 months.

Frequently asked questions

Do first home buyers pay stamp duty in NSW?

Eligible first home buyers pay no transfer duty on a home up to $800,000, and a reduced rate between $800,000 and $1,000,000. Vacant land is exempt up to $350,000.

When do I pay stamp duty in NSW?

Generally within 3 months of exchanging contracts. Your conveyancer or solicitor usually arranges payment at settlement.

Is there extra stamp duty for foreign buyers?

Yes — foreign persons pay a surcharge purchaser duty on top of standard transfer duty. If you're buying on a visa or from overseas, ask us how it applies to you.
